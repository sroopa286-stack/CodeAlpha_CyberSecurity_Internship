# Facilitator Notes

## Suggested 5-minute delivery

1. Introduce phishing as a message that tries to trick someone into sharing information, sending money, or opening a harmful attachment.
2. Show the suspicious email. Point out the look-alike sender, urgency, unexpected link, and request for a password.
3. Show the fake website signs. Emphasize that a padlock alone does not prove a website is legitimate.
4. Explain common social-engineering tactics: urgency, authority, fear, curiosity, and reward.
5. Close with the safe response: pause, verify through a trusted channel, report, and delete.
6. Ask learners to take the quiz.

## Quiz answer key

1. **B**: Verify through the organization’s official website or known contact details.
2. **B**: `support@paypaI.example` uses a capital `I` to resemble a lowercase `l`.
3. **C**: A padlock indicates encrypted transport, not that the organization is genuine.
4. **B**: A genuine IT team should not ask for your password by email.
5. **B**: Report the message using the organization’s process, then delete it.
